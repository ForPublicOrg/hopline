Hopline is group chat for places with no mobile signal and no WiFi: treks, camps, festivals, fairs, rallies, power cuts.

Phones link to each other directly over Bluetooth and WiFi, and every phone passes messages along for the others. A message can travel down a whole line of hikers or across a packed ground, far beyond the reach of any single phone.

No internet. No mobile signal. No account. No server. No hotspot to set up.

HOW IT WORKS
- One person starts a group and gets a 3-word code, like "tiger river lamp".
- Everyone else types the three words (or scans the QR). Done.
- A familiar chat app: group chat, private chats, photos and small files.
- Phones find each other on their own. Walk away and come back - the chat catches up by itself.

PHOTOS AND FILES
Tap the paperclip. Photos are shrunk hard (about 0.3 MB) so they hop in seconds; files up to 2 MB travel in pieces and are reassembled on every phone, even ones that were out of range when you sent them.

VOICE NOTES
Tap the mic, talk, send. Clips are tiny and hop the mesh like photos do - the play bar fills in as the pieces arrive.

LOCATION - EVEN LIVE
Send where you are (GPS works with no signal) or a meeting point from coordinates or a Google Maps link. Everyone sees how far the pin is and which way ("1.2 km away, north-east of you"); tapping opens the maps app. Or share live location for 15 minutes, an hour or 8 hours - it rides the beacons phones already send, and the People screen shows everyone who is sharing, updating as you walk.

REPLIES, REACTIONS, MENTIONS
Swipe a message to reply with a quote. Long-press any message for a reaction bar right over it - or + for every emoji - and double-tap to send a heart. Tap the reactions under a message to see who reacted. Type @ to mention someone - being mentioned always buzzes, even when the message arrives late as backlog.

NAMES, MUTE, TIDY CHATS
Change your name any time and everyone sees it, on your old messages too. Rename the group for everyone from Group info. Mute a chat, delete a message for yourself, clear a chat, reply privately, and reply straight from the notification. Chats open at the first unread message.

MORE THAN ONE GROUP
Keep a group for the trek, one for the family, one for the festival crew. Switching is one tap, and each group keeps its own history, unread counts and files.

HONEST TICKS
A clock while it waits for a phone in range, one tick when it is on its way, two ticks when phones confirm. A photo's tick only appears when the whole photo is on the other phone. Tap your own message to see exactly who has it, by name.

BUILT FOR A FEW FRIENDS OR A CROWD
In a small group every phone confirms every message. In a crowd of hundreds or thousands the phones automatically stop chattering about receipts, slow their "I am here" beacons, and switch photos off, so the radios carry your words instead of overhead.

SHARED INTERNET
If anyone in the group gets signal, everyone can use a sliver of it: the weather for exactly where you are, a text home ("I'm OK", with your location) sent by any phone with even one bar of mobile service, a quick look-up, or a web page as clean readable text. Ask even when nobody has signal - the request travels with the group and runs the moment someone gets it; if that phone goes quiet, another takes over. Answers come back only to you. Your phone helps only while "Share my internet" is on, within a daily allowance you choose, and never while roaming unless you allow it.

LIMITS, STATED PLAINLY
- Each hop is Bluetooth range: about 20-40 m in the open.
- Android only. iPhones cannot join.
- No end-to-end encryption - anyone with the 3-word code is in the group, and every phone in it carries private chats to pass them along.
- Bluetooth is flaky; links can take up to a minute to form.
- Media is deliberately small: photos ~0.3 MB, files up to 2 MB.

Open source (MIT). Install it on every phone before you need it.
